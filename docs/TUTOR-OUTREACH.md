# Signing your first four tutors

Four tutors with five students each is the whole $4,000 plan. This is not a
marketing problem, it is four conversations.

## The offer, in one line

> Keep 100% of your rate, stop hunting for students, and earn $100 every time
> one of yours signs up.

Hold that rate card. Do not negotiate it individually. Tutors talk to each
other, and a published rate reads as a real business while haggling reads as a
cheap one.

| | |
|---|---|
| Their tutoring rate | they keep all of it, we take nothing |
| Per Complete signup on their code | $100, paid monthly |
| Per live group review they run | $200 |
| Requirement | 515+, some tutoring experience |

## Where to find them

In rough order of how well they convert:

1. **People you already know** who scored well. One warm introduction beats
   fifty cold emails.
2. **Your own university** — the premed society, and whoever ran MCAT help
   sessions two years above you.
3. **r/MCAT and r/premed** — do not spam. Post once, honestly, as a founder
   looking for tutors, and answer every reply yourself.
4. **Wyzant and similar** — tutors there are already paying for client
   acquisition with their time, which is exactly the pain this fixes.

Send these one at a time, from your own address, with the name filled in. Four
personal emails will out-recruit four thousand automated ones, which is why
there is no bot here.

## Email 1 — someone you know

> Subject: would you tutor MCAT with me?
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I've built an MCAT platform — 250 lessons, 1,250 questions, all of it
> source-checked — and I'm looking for a few tutors to work with.
>
> You'd keep your whole rate. I don't take a cut of your sessions. What I pay
> for is referrals: $100 whenever a student of yours signs up, and $200 for
> each group review session you run before a test date.
>
> You'd also get a dashboard showing exactly which questions each of your
> students is getting wrong, so you're not spending paid hours re-teaching
> content they already have.
>
> Worth 15 minutes this week?
>
> Pedro

## Email 2 — a tutor you found online

> Subject: a question about how you take on students
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I saw you tutor MCAT. I'm not trying to sell you anything — I run a prep
> platform and I'm looking for a few tutors, and I'd rather ask than pitch.
>
> How much unpaid time do you spend finding students?
>
> That's the part I'm trying to take off tutors. You keep 100% of your rate —
> I take nothing from your sessions. I pay $100 when a student of yours signs
> up to the platform, and $200 for each group review you run.
>
> If the answer is "not much, I'm full", genuinely ignore this. If it's the
> usual answer, I'd like to talk.
>
> Pedro
> [link to /teach]

## Email 3 — the follow-up, about a week later

> Subject: re: tutoring with Vitalis
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Following up once, then I'll leave you alone.
>
> The short version: keep your rate, I take nothing, $100 per student who
> signs up on your code.
>
> If the timing's wrong, tell me and I'll check back after the next test date.

**Follow up once. Never twice.** A third email costs you the tutor and your
reputation in a small community.

## When one says yes

1. Their referral code is already generated by the application form.
2. Give them the code and the `/teach` terms in writing, in one email, so
   there is no ambiguity later about what they're owed.
3. Ask them to put their current students on it first. That is your fastest
   revenue — warm students who already trust them.
4. Track what you owe in the admin: referral codes are totalled there with the
   dollar amount against each.

## One thing to have ready before you sign anyone

Complete promises a live group review before every MCAT test date. If you sell
it and have no tutor to run one, you have sold something you cannot deliver.
Sign the first tutor before you push hard on Complete, or run the first
session yourself.
